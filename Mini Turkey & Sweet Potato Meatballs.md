# Mini Turkey & Sweet Potato Meatballs

**Serves:** Makes ~20 mini meatballs (2-3 toddler portions)
**Total time:** 50 minutes (20 active, 30 cooking/resting)
**Technique:** Roasting, hand-rolling
**Source:** Original — Pierre's Kitchen

---

Soft, tender, built for little hands. The sweet potato keeps these moist without any heavy egg-to-meat ratio, and the flavor is mild enough for a 13-month-old but interesting enough that you'll steal a few yourself. They freeze like a dream, and they're endlessly adaptable — swap the herbs, change the dip, bulk up the veg. Your kid already loves turkey in bolognese. This is just a new shape.

---

## Dietary Notes
- **Nut-free**, **peanut-free**, **lentil-free**, **pea-free** ✅
- **Dairy-free** as written — use dairy-free breadcrumbs
- *Egg:* Can substitute 1 flax egg (1 tbsp ground flax + 3 tbsp water) for egg-free version

---

## Mise en Place

### Meatball Base
- 1 lb ground turkey (93/7 preferred — enough fat to stay moist, lean enough to handle)
- 1 medium sweet potato (about 250g), roasted and mashed
- ¼ cup plain breadcrumbs (panko works fine; gluten-free breadcrumbs work too)
- 1 large egg, lightly beaten
- 2 cloves garlic, minced to paste
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, finely chopped
- 1 tsp kosher salt (Diamond Crystal; cut to ½ tsp if using Morton)
- ¼ tsp ground black pepper
- ¼ tsp ground cumin (optional — adds warmth without spice)

### Roasted Sweet Potato
- 1 medium sweet potato, scrubbed
- 1 tsp olive oil

### For Dipping (Optional)
- ¼ cup whole milk yogurt (or dairy-free yogurt)
- Pinch of minced fresh dill or mint

## Execution

### Step 1: Roast the Sweet Potato

Preheat oven to **400°F**. Prick the sweet potato several times with a fork. Rub with **1 tsp olive oil** and place directly on the oven rack or on a small lined sheet pan.

Roast **25-30 minutes** until completely tender — a knife should slide through the thickest part with zero resistance. **This isn't a step you rush: undercooked sweet potato won't mash smooth and will leave hard bits in the meatballs.**

Let cool until safe to handle — about **5 minutes**. Peel the skin off (it'll slip right off at this stage) and mash the flesh in a bowl until smooth. Measure **½ cup** of mashed sweet potato for the meatballs; eat the rest or save it.

Reduce oven temperature to **375°F**.

### Step 2: Mix and Roll

In a large bowl, combine:
- 1 lb ground turkey
- ½ cup mashed sweet potato
- ¼ cup breadcrumbs
- 1 beaten egg
- 2 cloves garlic paste
- 2 tbsp parsley
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- ¼ tsp cumin (if using)

Mix with your hands until **just combined**. Overworking ground turkey makes it dense — you want it cohesive but not compacted. **The mixture will feel quite soft and a little sticky. That's correct. It means tender meatballs.**

Line a sheet pan with parchment or foil. Roll mixture into **1-inch diameter balls** — about the size of a large grape but a little rounder. You should get **18-22 meatballs**. Space them 1 inch apart on the tray.

### Step 3: Bake

Bake at **375°F** for **15-17 minutes**. At 15 minutes, cut one open: it should be cooked through with no pink, but still visibly moist.

**Do not overcook.** Turkey gets dry faster than beef. 15 minutes is usually perfect for this size.

Let rest on the tray **5 minutes**. The carryover heat finishes the interior and the meatballs firm up enough to handle without falling apart.

### Step 4: Dip (Optional)

While meatballs rest, stir together **¼ cup yogurt** with a pinch of **dill or mint**. This gives a cool, familiar counterpoint — your kid already likes yogurt, so the dip is an entry point for the new texture.

---

## Plating

Arrange 4-6 meatballs on a plate with a small bowl of yogurt dip. Add soft-cooked carrot batons or avocado slices alongside for color and variety.

For older kids and adults: serve over rice orzo with a squeeze of lemon. You're making 20 — there's room for everyone.

---

## Chef's Notes

- **The sweet potato is doing crucial work here.** It replaces some of the fat that lean turkey lacks, adds natural sweetness kids respond to, and keeps the texture soft enough for early eaters' gums.
- **Taste the mixture raw (yes, with raw turkey) by cooking a tiny patty in a pan.** This is the only way to check your salt level before committing.
- **These freeze beautifully.** Arrange raw or cooked meatballs on a parchment-lined tray, freeze solid (2 hours), then transfer to a freezer bag. Raw: bake straight from frozen, add 5 minutes. Cooked: reheat at 350°F for 8-10 minutes.
- **Build your own:** Swap the parsley for basil or oregano. Add ¼ cup finely grated carrot or zucchini (squeeze out the moisture first). Use ground chicken or pork instead of turkey.

---

## Critical Notes

- **Don't overmix.** Turkey meatballs turn into hockey pucks when overworked. Mix until just combined — not a second more.
- **Don't skip the sweet potato cooling step.** Hot mashed sweet potato will partially cook the egg and turkey, creating a weird texture.
- **Moisture is your friend.** If the mixture feels dry (depends on the sweet potato's water content), add 1 tbsp of water or broth. If it feels too wet, add a tablespoon more breadcrumbs.
- **Freeze in a single layer first** or they'll fuse into one mega-meatball clump.

---

## Variations

- **No breadcrumbs:** Substitute ¼ cup cooked quinoa or finely ground oats
- **Egg-free:** 1 flax egg (1 tbsp ground flax + 3 tbsp water, let sit 5 minutes)
- **Dairy-free dip:** Blend ¼ cup canned coconut milk (thick part only) with a pinch of salt and dill
- **Pasta night:** Roll slightly larger (1.5 inch) and serve over spaghetti with a simple tomato sauce
