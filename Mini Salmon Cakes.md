# Mini Salmon Cakes

**Serves:** Makes ~12 mini cakes (2-3 toddler portions)
**Total time:** 45 minutes (20 active, 25 cooking/resting)
**Technique:** Pan-frying, finishing in oven
**Source:** Original — Pierre's Kitchen

---

Bright, tender, and surprisingly mild. Canned salmon gets a bad rap — this is the recipe that changes minds. A little mashed potato binds everything without turning it into a fish cake that tastes like a baked potato. The pan fry gives you golden-brown crunch on the outside; the oven finish keeps the center delicate. Your kid gets omega-3s, iron, and a whole new protein category, all in something that looks like a crispy little pancake and fits perfectly in two tiny hands.

## Dietary Notes
- **Nut-free**, **peanut-free**, **lentil-free**, **pea-free** ✅
- **Dairy-free** as written
- *Egg:* Cakes rely on egg for binding — substitute is tricky; try 1 flax egg + extra panko for egg-sensitive kids
- **Contains fish** (salmon)

## Mise en Place

### Salmon Cake Base
- 1 can (6 oz / 170g) wild salmon, drained (skin and bones removed — or leave them mashed in for extra calcium)
- 1 small Russet or Yukon Gold potato (about 150g), peeled, boiled, and mashed
- 2 tbsp plain panko breadcrumbs (plus more for dredging)
- 1 large egg, lightly beaten
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard (mild — adds tang without heat)
- 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice (about ¼ lemon)
- 1 tbsp fresh dill, finely chopped (or 1 tsp dried)
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, finely chopped
- ¼ tsp kosher salt (Diamond Crystal)
- Pinch of black pepper

### For Cooking
- 2 tbsp olive oil or avocado oil (for pan-frying)

### Optional Yogurt Sauce
- ¼ cup whole milk yogurt (or dairy-free yogurt)
- ½ tsp lemon zest
- Pinch of salt

## Execution

### Step 1: Prep the Potato

Peel and quarter **1 small potato**. Boil in salted water until fork-tender — about **10-12 minutes**. Drain and return to the hot pot for **30 seconds** to steam off excess moisture. Mash thoroughly with a fork or potato masher — no lumps, no ricer needed.

**Why you steam-dry:** Wet potato makes the cakes fall apart. Getting rid of surface moisture after draining is the single most important binding step.

### Step 2: Mix the Cakes

Open the can of salmon. Drain well. Pick through for large bones (optional — they're soft and edible; some parents leave them for calcium) and remove any large dark skin pieces.

In a bowl, combine:
- Drained salmon, flaked
- ½ cup mashed potato (about the volume of the salmon)
- 2 tbsp panko
- 1 beaten egg
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 1 tbsp dill
- 2 tbsp parsley
- ¼ tsp salt
- Pinch of pepper

Mix gently with a fork until everything is evenly distributed. Don't mash it into a paste — you want distinct flakes of salmon and potato for texture.

### Step 3: Shape

Spread a few tablespoons of extra panko on a plate. Form the mixture into **1½-inch diameter patties**, about **½ inch thick**. You should get **10-12 cakes**.

Lightly press each cake into the panko on both sides — just enough for a thin crust. Set on a parchment-lined tray.

**The mixture is delicate at this stage. Don't sweat it. They firm up fast in the pan.**

### Step 4: Sear

Heat **2 tbsp oil** in a non-stick skillet over **medium heat** until shimmering — about **90 seconds**.

Gently place cakes in the pan without crowding. Cook **3-4 minutes** on the first side until deep golden brown. The cake should release easily when it's ready — if it sticks, give it another 30 seconds. The crust tells the story: golden, crisp, set.

Flip carefully with a thin spatula. Cook **3 minutes** on the second side.

### Step 5: Oven Finish

Transfer the skillet to a **375°F** oven (or transfer cakes to a sheet pan if your skillet isn't oven-safe). Bake **6-8 minutes** until heated through and firm to the touch.

This step ensures the centers are fully cooked without burning the exterior. It also gives you a minute to clean up and prep the sauce.

Let rest **5 minutes** on the tray. These hold heat well.

### Step 6: Yogurt Sauce

While the cakes rest, mix **¼ cup yogurt** with **½ tsp lemon zest** and a pinch of **salt**. Simple. Fresh. Done.

---

## Plating

Place 3-4 mini cakes on a plate with the yogurt sauce in a small ramekin. Add soft-cooked green beans or steamed carrot sticks alongside. For adults: serve on a bed of arugula with a lemon wedge and the same yogurt sauce.

---

## Chef's Notes

- **Canned salmon is a pantry superstar.** Wild-caught (Alaskan pink or red) has better flavor and omega-3 content than farmed Atlantic. It's already cooked — you're just warming it through.
- **Leave the bones in** if you can. They're soft, calcium-rich, and mash into the cakes so thoroughly that nobody notices. Seriously — just crush them with a fork.
- **The oven finish is non-negotiable for this size.** A pan-only approach either burns the crust before the center warms through, or leaves the crust pale and sad.
- **Make a double batch.** These freeze after cooking: cool completely, tray-freeze, then bag. Reheat at 350°F for 8 minutes from frozen.

---

## Critical Notes

- **Dry ingredients mean stable cakes.** Wet potato + undrained salmon = falling-apart cakes in the pan. Take the extra minute to drain and steam-dry.
- **Don't crowd the pan.** Oil temperature drops, cakes steam instead of sear, and you lose the crust. Batch cook if needed.
- **Flip gently.** These are more fragile than beef patties. A fish spatula (thin, slotted) is ideal. A regular spatula works — just be deliberate.
- **Cool to finger-safe before serving to a toddler.** The crust stays hot longer than the center. Give it 5 full minutes.

---

## Variations

- **Swap the fish:** Canned tuna works exactly the same way (skip the dill, use chives instead)
- **No potato:** Substitute ½ cup cooked and mashed cauliflower or parsnip
- **Egg-free (tricky, worth a shot):** 1 flax egg + 1 extra tbsp panko; they'll be more fragile, so handle with care
- **Herb swap:** Try basil + chive instead of dill + parsley for a different flavor profile
- **Crispy upgrade:** Add 1 tbsp finely minced red bell pepper for color and sweetness
